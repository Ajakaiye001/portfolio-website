# Closet Manager
### Style Your Life, Simplified. Your Wardrobe, Your Way.

---

## Project Overview

**Role:** UX Designer (Uchechi Kalu & Timilehin Ajakaiye)
**Type:** Mobile App · UX Case Study
**Deliverables:** Research, User Personas, Journey Map, User Flow, Visual Design, Hi-Fi Screens

Closet Manager is an AI-powered wardrobe assistant that takes the friction out of getting dressed. By blending smart outfit recommendations with calendar integration and laundry management, it gives users a personalised, effortless dressing experience, every single day.

---

## The Problem

Getting dressed sounds simple. In practice, it rarely is.

Between demanding work schedules, shifting social calendars, seasonal wardrobe transitions, and the mental load of keeping a closet organised, deciding what to wear can quickly become a daily source of stress. Most people aren't struggling because they lack clothes. They're struggling because they lack a system.

> **How might we help people move from a disorganised wardrobe to a confident, personalised dressing routine without adding complexity to their day?**

---

## Research

### Methodology

We conducted structured user interviews across a diverse group of potential users. The interviews were divided into five thematic sections:

- **General Usage & Habits:** How often do users struggle with outfit selection? What does that look like in practice?
- **Wardrobe & Style Preferences:** How do people currently organise their wardrobes? What factors influence their daily choices?
- **Calendar & Event Integration:** Do daily activities shape clothing decisions? Would calendar-linked outfit suggestions add value?
- **Laundry & Clothing Management:** How do users track clothing usage and rotation? Where do reminders fit in?
- **Technology & App Preferences:** What would make a wardrobe app genuinely worth using?

### Key Insights

Analysing the interview data revealed three core user motivations that shaped the entire design direction:

| User Need | Share | Insight |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| **Convenience** | 30% | Busy professionals and frequent travellers need fast, contextual outfit suggestions, often weather- and destination-aware. |
| **Efficiency** | 40% | Social influencers and fashion-forward users want a tool that helps them curate content, track trends, and style outfits systematically. |
| **Personalised Assistance** | 30% | Organisation-focused users want a clean wardrobe inventory with laundry management built in, simplifying their daily routine end-to-end. |

---

## User Segmentation

Two distinct user categories emerged from the research data, each with different primary goals.

### 1. Professional & Social Users

- **Corporate Professionals** who need appropriate, polished attire for formal settings, meetings, and business travel, with minimal decision fatigue.
- **Socially Active Users** attending parties, events, and gatherings who want confident, occasion-appropriate outfit picks without having to overthink it.

### 2. Fashion & Lifestyle Enthusiasts

- **Fashion Enthusiasts & Influencers** are trendsetters and content creators who want a tool that keeps them organised and fashion-forward, and makes curating shareable looks easier.
- **Lifestyle & Varied Users** covers students, parents, travellers, and fitness enthusiasts who each have unique wardrobe needs across different areas of their lives.

Grouping users into these broader segments allowed us to design with focus. Rather than trying to serve everyone identically, we could tailor features to genuinely address what each group cares most about.

Ridwan works in a fast-paced architectural firm where professional appearance is part of the culture. **Pain Points**

## User Flow

The app is structured around three primary flows, all accessible from a central Home screen:

```
START
  └── Onboarding → Sign Up / Sign In

HOME
  ├── Outfit of the Day
  │     └── Shuffle Outfit
  │
  ├── Outfits
  │     ├── Saved Outfits
  │     ├── Upload Outfit → Add Outfit Details → Save
  │     │     ├── Take Photo
  │     │     └── Upload from Gallery
  │
  ├── Events
  │     ├── Upcoming Events
  │     ├── Add Event → Event Details → Save
  │     └── Saved Events
  │
  └── Profile
        ├── Clothing Settings
        ├── Laundry Settings
        ├── Notifications
        ├── Password
        └── Send Feedback / Suggestions
```

The flow was designed around one core principle: users should be able to accomplish any task, from viewing today's outfit to scheduling an event, in three taps or fewer.

## Key Features

### 1. Outfit of the Day
The app opens with a personalised daily outfit curated around the user's upcoming schedule, stated preferences, and current trends. Users can shuffle for an alternative or save looks they love.

### 2. Scan & Upload Outfits
Users photograph and tag their clothing with details like colour, style, category, and occasion. This builds a personal wardrobe database that powers increasingly accurate recommendations over time.

### 3. Events & Calendar Integration
Users add events directly in-app, from formal presentations to casual outings. Closet Manager surfaces outfit suggestions tailored to each occasion. With Google Calendar and Apple Calendar integration, the app can read the user's existing schedule and proactively prepare outfit options before they even think to ask.

### 4. Weather API Integration
Outfit suggestions factor in real-time weather data, which is particularly useful for frequent travellers who need destination-aware packing guidance.

### 5. Laundry Management
An often-overlooked part of wardrobe management. Users can track clothing usage, schedule wash cycles, and set reminders for dry cleaning or specialist care, keeping the wardrobe fresh and the algorithm accurate.

## Reflection

This project reinforced a few things that matter deeply in UX work.

**Research shapes everything.** The three motivations we uncovered (convenience, efficiency, and personalised assistance) ended up driving every design decision, from the home screen hierarchy down to feature prioritisation.

**Segmentation enables focus.** Rather than designing a generic wardrobe app, grouping users into two primary segments gave us a clear lens for evaluating every feature and flow. It kept us honest.

**The small things are the product.** The three-tap rule, the shuffle button, the laundry reminders. These aren't just features, they're moments of respect for the user's time. And that's what makes an app worth coming back to.
